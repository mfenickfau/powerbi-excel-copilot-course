This is just my version. These are my own takes on three sets of learning outcomes for AI-focused training: Power BI, Excel and Copilot. I designed the course structure, wrote the slides, demos and labs, and built the practice data. It is independent work, not official material from Microsoft or any training provider.

How every topic is taught

The same pattern runs through all 169 topics, so learners always know what comes next and instructors can drop any topic in or out. Most of the time is spent doing, not watching. There is more lab material than any one delivery needs: instructors run Part A in class and choose Part B labs and challenges to fit the time and licences, and the rest is follow-up practice.

1

Explain

One slide: what the feature is, how it works and why it exists.

2

Demo

A step-by-step demo script in the speaker notes, run live on the practice data.

3

Lab, Part A and B

Part A is the core lab. Part B takes the same skill further on a harder, more realistic task.

4

Challenge

Each module ends with a business scenario that combines its topics. Every lab ends with a check: AI drafts, people verify.

Practice data with answers planted in it

All labs use Brightline Outdoor Co., a fictional outdoor-gear retailer. The data is synthetic and built so the AI has something real to find, and learners can check whether it did.

Sales and returns

Apparel, footwear, online orders and deep discounts drive returns. A store closes for a month; an outage and Black Friday show up as anomalies.

Documents and email

A flawed report with three planted errors, a meeting transcript, email threads with a buried ask, and two contract versions that differ in six places.

Safety traps

An untrusted workbook and an email with hidden instructions aimed at AI, for practising prompt-injection awareness.

Feature status was researched in November 2026. Topics marked verify describe features that were new, renamed, in preview or rolling out, and should be checked before teaching.
